Baby massage in Dubai: classes, safe oils and how to start at home

Baby massage in Dubai is mostly something parents learn rather than book: a short course of classes, then ten calm minutes at home. Here is what the evidence says, which oils to use, how classes work and when to check with your paediatrician first.

What infant massage is, and who does the massaging

Infant massage is slow, gentle stroking of a baby's legs, tummy, chest, arms, back and face, done with the hands and a little oil. The point is not to work on muscles. It is closer to a structured cuddle: steady touch, eye contact, your voice, and a predictable sequence the baby learns to recognise. Most routines start at the legs and feet because babies tolerate touch there more easily, then move up the body, leaving the face for last.

Massaging babies is an old habit in many of the cultures that make up Dubai. In South Asian households a grandmother or a hired helper often massages the newborn with oil every day before the bath. Arab, Filipino and African families have their own versions. The modern class format, popularised by organisations such as the International Association of Infant Massage, adds structure: a set of named strokes, guidance on reading the baby's cues, and an emphasis on the parent doing the massage rather than a stranger.

That last point matters. You will see a baby massage therapist in Dubai advertised now and then, offering to come to the house and massage the baby. A professional can be useful as a teacher. For the massage itself, the research and the instructors both lean towards parents and carers doing it, because so much of the value comes from the interaction.

What the research actually shows

It is worth being honest here, because baby massage is often sold with big promises. For healthy, full-term babies, a Cochrane review of the trials found some signs of benefit for sleep, crying and parent-infant interaction, but the studies were mostly small and at high risk of bias. The reviewers did not find strong enough evidence to recommend it as a health intervention for low-risk babies. Treat it as a pleasant, low-risk routine that many families enjoy, not as therapy.

The evidence is stronger for premature babies. Trials in neonatal units have linked gentle, structured massage to better weight gain and shorter hospital stays, although the quality varies and this is done under medical supervision. If your baby was born early, ask the neonatal team or your paediatrician before you start at home.

Colic is the question every exhausted parent asks about. Some small studies suggest massage may reduce crying time, others show no difference. A gentle tummy routine (clockwise circles, knees-to-tummy) may help some babies pass wind, and it does no harm to try, but persistent, inconsolable crying, poor feeding or vomiting needs a doctor, not a technique.

When to start, and how to read your baby

Most classes in Dubai take babies from around six weeks, partly because by then the six-week check is done and mothers are a little more mobile. At home, you can start gentle leg and back strokes in the first weeks if your baby is healthy, avoiding the area around the umbilical stump until it has fallen off and the skin has healed. Keep early sessions very short, a few minutes is plenty.

Choosing the moment

Pick a time when the baby is awake, fed but not just fed, and not overtired. Around 30 to 45 minutes after a feed works for many families, and a session before the evening bath becomes a useful bedtime cue. Warm the room, because air conditioning set to 21°C feels very different to a naked baby than to you, and put a towel down in case of accidents.

Signs to stop

Babies tell you clearly when they have had enough: turning the head away, arching the back, splayed fingers, hiccups, crying or going suddenly still. Stop and simply hold them. A good instructor will spend as much time on these cues as on the strokes themselves.

Which oil to use on newborn skin

Oil choice deserves more thought than it usually gets. A newborn's skin barrier is still maturing, and a UK study found that olive oil applied to newborn skin could weaken that barrier, which is why many midwives now advise against it. Mustard oil, traditional in parts of South Asia, has similar concerns and can irritate. Sunflower oil high in linoleic acid is the option most often suggested by researchers, and many instructors recommend a plain, unscented, cold-pressed vegetable oil or a fragrance-free product made for babies.

A few other ground rules:

  • Do a patch test on a small area of the leg and wait a day before using a new oil
  • Avoid peanut oil, and talk to your doctor before using other nut oils if there is food allergy or eczema in the family
  • Skip essential oils entirely for young babies; they can irritate skin and airways
  • Use very little oil, and keep it away from the eyes and hands that go into the mouth

If your baby has eczema or very dry skin, ask your paediatrician or dermatologist which emollient to use instead. The aromatic oils used for adults on our aromatherapy massage page are not suitable here, and nor are the medicated oils from Ayurvedic massage unless a doctor familiar with them has advised it for your child.

Baby massage classes in Dubai: what happens

A typical baby massage course in Dubai runs over four or five weekly sessions of about an hour, in a small group of five to eight parents and babies. You sit on mats with your baby on a towel in front of you, and the instructor demonstrates on a doll while you follow along with your own child. Each week adds a body area, so by the end you have a full routine of around 15 minutes. There is a lot of pausing for feeds, nappy changes and naps, and good instructors plan for that.

Classes are run by children's wellness centres, family clinics, community centres, some hotels and independent instructors who hire a room. Areas with many young families, such as Jumeirah, Al Barsha, Arabian Ranches, Mirdif, Dubai Hills and Jumeirah Village Circle, tend to have the most options. Some sessions are mothers only, which suits women who prefer not to breastfeed in front of men; ask when booking.

Many centres run classes in the morning on weekdays, and they tend to fill a few weeks ahead, especially from September to May when families are back from summer. If getting out of the house with a newborn feels like too much, private lessons at home are widely offered and cover the same ground in one or two longer visits.

Prices: courses, workshops and home lessons

Prices shift and vary by provider, so confirm the current rate when you book. As a rough guide, expect around AED 400–1,000 for a four or five week group course, with oil and handouts sometimes included. One-off workshops of 60 to 90 minutes typically cost about AED 150–350. A private lesson at home usually runs from roughly AED 300–600 per visit, and some instructors offer a two-visit package.

Bundles are common. Some postnatal centres combine a session for you with a lesson for the baby, often sold as mother and baby massage. Our pregnancy and postnatal massage page explains what the parent's side of that involves. Health insurance rarely covers baby massage classes, though a few clinic-run programmes for premature babies sit within medical care.

Choosing an instructor and staying safe

Ask what training the instructor has and who certified it. An internationally recognised infant massage certification, along with a background in nursing, midwifery, physiotherapy or early years work, is a good sign. They should ask about your baby's age, birth history, health conditions and recent vaccinations, and should be happy to tell you when not to massage. Our general guide to how to choose a massage centre covers licensing and red flags; the centres listed below run legitimate, professional classes.

Be cautious of anyone who claims massage will cure reflux, colic, jaundice or developmental delays, or who uses firm pressure on joints or the spine. Infant massage should never involve cracking, stretching limbs forcefully or pressing on the soft spot on the head. Treatments like reflexology are sometimes marketed for babies; gentle foot stroking is fine, but there is no good evidence that pressing points on the feet treats any infant condition.

When not to massage

Skip it, and speak to your paediatrician if needed, when your baby has a fever, a rash or broken skin, is unwell or unusually sleepy, or has recently had surgery. After vaccinations, leave the injection site alone for a day or two and keep the session gentle. Babies with heart conditions, bone or joint problems, or who were born very early should only be massaged with medical advice.

Baby Massage: common questions

At what age can I start baby massage?

Gentle strokes on the legs and back can start in the first weeks if your baby is healthy, avoiding the umbilical stump until it has healed. Most baby massage classes in Dubai accept babies from about six weeks, after the routine postnatal check, until they start crawling.

Which oil is best for baby massage?

A plain, unscented vegetable oil such as high-linoleic sunflower oil, or a fragrance-free baby product, is the usual recommendation. Avoid olive and mustard oil on newborn skin, avoid peanut oil, skip essential oils, and patch test anything new. Ask your paediatrician if your baby has eczema.

How much do baby massage classes cost in Dubai?

Expect roughly AED 400 to 1,000 for a four or five week group course, around AED 150 to 350 for a single workshop, and AED 300 to 600 for a private lesson at home. Prices change, so confirm the current rate when booking.

Does baby massage help with colic?

The evidence is mixed. Some small studies found less crying, others found no difference. Gentle tummy strokes may help some babies settle or pass wind and are low risk to try, but persistent crying, feeding problems or vomiting should be checked by a doctor.

Should I hire a therapist to massage my baby?

Most instructors recommend that parents or regular carers do the massage, since much of the benefit comes from the interaction itself. A qualified instructor is useful for teaching you a safe routine, whether in a group class or a private lesson at home.
