Balinese and Lomi Lomi massage in Dubai: a resident's guide

Balinese massage in Dubai has become a menu staple, and Lomi Lomi is following close behind. Both are oil-based and flowing, but they feel quite different on the table. Here is what each involves, what you will pay, and how to choose well.

Two island traditions, one shared table

Balinese massage comes from Indonesia and blends several influences: Indian Ayurvedic oil work, Chinese acupressure and the local practice of urut, a firm village-style rub passed down through families. A typical session mixes long strokes with thumb pressure on specific points, skin rolling, and the occasional gentle stretch. It sits somewhere between a relaxing oil massage and a more purposeful treatment.

Lomi Lomi is Hawaiian. Traditionally it was part of a wider practice of healing and family reconciliation, and the Hawaiian massage you will find on spa menus focuses on long, continuous strokes made with the forearms as well as the hands. The therapist often works across two areas of the body at once, for instance from your shoulder down to the opposite hip, which is why people describe it as feeling like waves. The pace is slower and the pressure generally lighter.

Bamboo and other variations

Some Dubai spas offer bamboo massage as a Balinese-style add-on, rolling warm bamboo canes of different widths over oiled muscles. It sounds gimmicky but can be pleasant on the thighs and back, where the rolling gives a broad, even pressure. Others pair Balinese technique with heated herbal compresses, small cloth bundles of ginger, lemongrass and turmeric pressed along the back.

What happens during the session

You will fill in a brief health form at reception, then be shown to a treatment room that is usually dim, warm and scented with frangipani or lemongrass oil. The therapist leaves while you undress to your comfort level and lie face down under a towel. Disposable underwear is normally offered. Only the area being worked on is uncovered at any time.

Many centres start with a short foot ritual: warm water, a little salt, maybe flower petals. In a Balinese session the therapist then works through the back with a combination of gliding strokes and firm thumb pressure, moving to the legs, then asking you to turn over for the arms, front of the legs, shoulders and scalp. Lomi Lomi uses more oil and longer, sweeping strokes, and the towel may be draped a little differently so the therapist can work in one continuous line from shoulder to foot.

Expect to feel drowsy afterwards. Most centres let you sit for a few minutes with tea or water. The oil will leave your skin soft and slightly shiny, so if you have plans afterwards, ask whether there is a shower, or bring a change of clothes you don't mind getting a little oily.

Is it right for you?

Balinese massage suits people who want relaxation with some substance. If a classic Swedish massage feels too light but you don't want the intensity of deep tissue work, it is a sensible middle ground. Lomi Lomi is better when your main aim is to slow down: after a hectic week of school runs on Sheikh Zayed Road or quarter-end deadlines, its rhythm can be genuinely calming.

As with most oil massages, research supports short-term benefits for perceived stress, mood and muscle tension, and some studies link regular massage with better sleep. The spiritual ideas behind Lomi Lomi, or the energy points in Balinese practice, come from tradition rather than clinical evidence. That does not make them worthless, it just means the measurable effect is relaxation and comfort, not treatment of any condition.

Neither style is ideal for a specific injury, a stiff shoulder after a fall, or sports recovery. For those, a therapist with remedial training will help more.

Balinese massage versus Thai, aromatherapy and hot stone

The most common confusion is with Thai massage. Thai is done clothed on a mat with no oil and plenty of assisted stretching; Balinese happens on a table with oil and only light stretches. If you want to stay dressed and be moved around, choose Thai. If you want warm oil and a slower pace, choose Balinese.

An aromatherapy massage is gentler and built around the choice of essential oils, while a hot stone massage uses heated basalt to soften muscles before the therapist works. Lomi Lomi feels closest to a very fluid Swedish massage, just with forearms doing more of the work.

Prices and formats in Dubai

As a rough 2026 guide, independent wellness centres typically charge around AED 180–300 for 60 minutes of Balinese massage and AED 250–400 for 90 minutes. Mid-range spas tend to sit between AED 300 and AED 450 for an hour. In five-star hotel spas expect roughly AED 450–650 or more, often with access to steam rooms and a relaxation lounge. Lomi Lomi is usually priced slightly higher, as fewer therapists are properly trained in it. Prices shift often, so confirm the total, including VAT, before you book.

Home service and deals

Several licensed home-service providers offer a Bali massage in Dubai apartments and villas, bringing a folding table, towels and oils. It is convenient, though the atmosphere depends on you dimming the lights and switching off the television. Weekday daytime slots and multi-session packages are where the best prices usually appear. Some spas also bundle Balinese massage with a scrub or a short facial at a combined rate.

Finding a skilled Balinese or Lomi Lomi therapist

Many therapists offering Indonesian massage in Dubai are Balinese or Javanese themselves and trained either in Bali's spa schools or in large resort spas before moving here. Others learned the style on a short course. Both can be good, but ask how long they have practised and where they trained. If you want Lomi Lomi massage in Dubai, it is worth asking whether the therapist completed a dedicated Lomi Lomi course rather than simply adding forearm strokes to a standard routine.

Look for a clean, quiet room, clear pricing and a consultation that goes beyond ticking boxes. Reviews mentioning a specific therapist by name are a good sign. Location matters for how often you will actually go: residents in Jumeirah, Umm Suqeim and Al Barsha have a wide choice of mid-range spas, while the Marina, JBR and Downtown lean towards hotel spas. Legitimate centres display their trade licence and price list; anything advertised through cards on windscreens is not what this guide covers. Our guide to choosing a massage centre goes into licensing in more detail, and the centres listed below have been checked.

Safety and contraindications

Oil massage is gentle on most bodies, but tell your therapist about allergies, especially to nuts, since some blends use almond or macadamia oil. Skin conditions, open cuts, recent sunburn and varicose veins should be mentioned so those areas can be avoided. If you are pregnant, book a specific prenatal treatment rather than a standard Balinese session, and check with your doctor first.

People with heart conditions, blood clotting disorders, recent surgery or uncontrolled blood pressure should speak to their doctor before booking. If you feel lightheaded when getting up from the table, sit for a minute, drink water and tell the therapist.

Local tips worth knowing

In summer, the walk from a car park can leave you overheated before you even reach reception, so give yourself 15 minutes to cool down and drink something before the session. Avoid booking straight after a large meal or a hard workout.

During Ramadan, hours usually shift later, and some spas run evening offers after iftar. Weekend slots, particularly Friday afternoon and Saturday, fill several days ahead. If you prefer a female therapist, many centres can arrange this, and some have separate ladies' areas; mention it when you book. A modest tip of around AED 20–50 is appreciated but not mandatory, and hotel spas often add a service charge already.

Balinese & Lomi Lomi Massage: common questions

What is the difference between Balinese and Lomi Lomi massage?

Balinese massage combines oil strokes with firm thumb pressure on specific points, skin rolling and light stretching. Lomi Lomi, from Hawaii, uses long, continuous forearm strokes that often cross the body, with lighter pressure and a slower rhythm. Balinese feels more purposeful; Lomi Lomi feels more flowing and sleepy.

How much does a Balinese massage cost in Dubai?

Expect roughly AED 180–300 for an hour at an independent centre, AED 300–450 at a mid-range spa and AED 450–650 or more in a hotel spa. Lomi Lomi is usually priced a little higher. Prices change frequently, so confirm the final figure, including VAT and any service charge, when you book.

Is Balinese massage good for back pain?

It can ease general muscle tension and help you relax, which some people find reduces everyday back discomfort. It is not a treatment for a specific injury, disc problem or nerve pain. If your pain is sharp, persistent or follows an accident, see a doctor or physiotherapist before booking any massage.

What is bamboo massage?

Bamboo massage uses warm, polished bamboo canes of different sizes, rolled and pressed over oiled muscles. It is often offered alongside Balinese technique in Dubai spas. The canes give broad, even pressure and suit the back and thighs. It is a pleasant variation rather than a distinct medical technique.

Can I get a Balinese massage at home in Dubai?

Yes. Several licensed home-service providers bring a folding table, towels and oils to apartments and villas. Prices are similar to mid-range spas. Prepare a quiet room with space around the table, dim the lights and keep the air conditioning comfortable rather than cold.
