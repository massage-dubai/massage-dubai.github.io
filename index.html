Questions people ask before booking

What is the most popular type of massage in Dubai?

Swedish and Thai massage are the two most booked styles across Dubai, followed by deep tissue for people with desk or gym-related tension. Swedish suits a first massage because the pressure is adjustable and oil-based. Thai is done clothed on a mat and feels more active. Our type pages explain how each one differs.

How much does a massage cost in Dubai?

For a 60-minute session in 2026, expect roughly AED 150 to 300 at neighbourhood day centres, AED 300 to 550 at mid-range and mall spas, and AED 550 to 1,000 or more at five-star hotel spas. Home service usually costs about the same as a mid-range spa. Weekday mornings and packages are often cheaper. Always confirm the price when booking.

Can I get a massage at home in Dubai?

Yes. Several licensed companies send therapists to homes and hotel rooms across Dubai, usually with a portable table, towels and oils. Book through the company's website or app rather than a private number, check the therapist's name and the price in advance, and expect a minimum booking of 60 minutes in most areas.

How can I tell if a massage centre in Dubai is legitimate?

A legitimate centre has a fixed address, displays its trade licence, publishes a menu with prices, asks you to fill in a short health form and keeps you properly draped with towels. Be wary of anything promoted through cards left on cars, WhatsApp-only bookings or prices far below the market. Our guide on choosing a centre covers this in detail.

Which massage is best for back pain?

For ordinary muscular back pain, deep tissue, sports or a focused back, neck and shoulder massage usually helps most. If the pain is sharp, follows an injury, travels down a leg or comes with numbness, see a doctor or physiotherapist first. A clinic offering medical or therapeutic massage can then work alongside your treatment plan.
